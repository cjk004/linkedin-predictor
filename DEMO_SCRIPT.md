# Demo talking points (about 90 seconds)

Have the app running and the browser maximized before you hit record. Leave the preset on "Example from the assignment" so it opens on the 42 year old.

**0:00 - What it is (10 sec)**
"This is the LinkedIn predictor from my final project. The marketing team describes a customer on the left, and the logistic regression model from Part 1 says whether that person is likely on LinkedIn and gives the probability."

**0:10 - The assignment example (15 sec)**
Point at the green box and the 72%. "This is the person from the instructions: high income, some grad school, married woman, 42, no kids at home. 72%, so she's classified as a user, and the meter shows her well above the 34% survey average."

**0:25 - Age (15 sec)**
Drag the age slider to 82. Box goes red, 45%. "Same person at 82 and she flips to not a user. The curve on the left is this exact profile at every age. For her it crosses 50% at 76, so even for affluent, educated customers LinkedIn is a working-age channel."

**0:40 - Change one thing (10 sec)**
Point at the bar chart on the right. "The what-if chart moves one input at a time. Education and income are the biggest levers, and each step is worth about 5 to 6 points for this person."

**0:50 - Segments and platforms (20 sec)**
Click the second tab. "This is the survey itself, not the model. The heatmap shows where LinkedIn users are concentrated - the outlined cell is our person's segment, 30 to 44 and $75k plus, where 60% use LinkedIn. And on the right, what else that segment uses. LinkedIn is the third most used platform for them, behind only YouTube and Facebook, and 60% versus 34% for the survey overall. So for this segment it's a strong channel. Switch to education and the story is the same."

**1:10 - Presets (10 sec)**
Pick "Retiree" from the preset dropdown. Box goes red, 28%. "Presets let the team jump between customer types. A 70 year old with an associate degree is at 28%. Only 8% of his segment is on LinkedIn - they're on YouTube and Facebook, and LinkedIn drops to sixth."

**1:20 - About the model (10 sec)**
Click the third tab. "Last tab is the evidence. 66% accuracy on held out data, but the number that matters is recall: it finds about two thirds of the actual LinkedIn users, and half the people it flags really are users against a 34% base rate. That's the app."
